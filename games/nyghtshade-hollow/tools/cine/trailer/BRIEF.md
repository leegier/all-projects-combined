# Nyghtshade Hollow trailer: production brief

Everything here is real in-engine footage of the playable slice, rendered deterministically through `tools/cine/render.mjs` (software GL, 1920x804 scope, 24 fps) and assembled with ffmpeg. Nothing is mocked up, and no claim is made that the game does not do.

## The owner's rules (non-negotiable)

1. **Humans.** The owner requires MetaHuman-quality realistic humans. No such characters exist in the game yet (the MetaHuman packages need an Unreal export on the owner's PC), and the procedural stand-ins look like clay mannequins. **This cut must therefore not show them**: no faces, no front or three-quarter views of people, no crowds in focus, no dialogue portraits. Allowed: empty spaces (stage option `humans: 'hide'`), the first-person hands (bot shots with `cam: 'player'`), objects, doors, signs, lights, the UI, and *backlit silhouettes seen from behind at 4 m or more with depth of field* (use sparingly, only where a person is essential). When MetaHumans arrive this trailer is re-rendered with a different cast; shots should be built so that is simply a re-render.
2. **Honesty.** Only what the game really has. Text that appears on screen uses lines that exist in `src/story/dialogue.js`, `src/story/cinematics.js`, `src/systems/routine.js` (PA lines) or the README.
3. **Cinematic quality, "nothing low-budget":** composed frames, motivated light, depth, deliberate camera moves, clean pacing. Every shot must look like it was framed by a person.
4. **Visual only.** Nothing in this work may change the simulation, its tests or the playable game. Do NOT edit anything under `src/` (an engine need goes into your report as an `engineRequests` entry; work around it meanwhile). Do NOT run `npm run build*`, `git commit`, or `git push`. Other agents render at the same time on the same 4 cores.

## Timeline (24 fps, 90 BPM: 1 beat = 16 frames, 1 bar = 64 frames, 52 bars = 3328 frames = 138.7 s)

Defined in `tools/cine/trailer/timeline.mjs` (read it: ranges, frame budgets and a one-line mood per range). `node tools/cine/trailer/check.mjs` verifies that the shots of every range add up to its budget **exactly** and that every shot length is a multiple of 8 frames (half a beat), so cuts land on the music. Hits of the score fall on the first frame of bars 5, 11, 17, 24, 38 and 48; the silence of the drop is bar 23. Shot ids are `<range id>-<nn>-<name>`, e.g. `A1-03-routine-board`, so they sort in edit order.

## Tools

* Shot renderer: `node tools/cine/render.mjs FILE.mjs --out DIR [--only id1,prefix*] [--scout --scale 0.5] [--workers 2]`. **Always run it exactly like this** (own build snapshot, own port range, software GL):
  `NH_DIST=/tmp/claude-0/-home-user/2b52fcc0-c3a1-5708-8b30-8de2fcf92246/scratchpad/dist-trailer NH_PORT=<your base port> NH_GL=llvmpipe xvfb-run -a -s "-screen 0 2000x1000x24" node tools/cine/render.mjs ...` from `/home/user/all-projects-combined/games/nyghtshade-hollow`. Scouting renders three stills per shot (start / middle / end) into `DIR/scout/<id>_{a,b,c}.png` and a contact sheet `DIR/<sheet>.jpg`; look at the PNGs with the Read tool. About 25 s per shot at `--scale 0.5` with two workers when the machine is quiet; use `--scale 1` for a full-resolution check of hero shots. Each shot starts in a fresh page, so shots are independent.
* Read before writing shots: `tools/cine/lib.mjs` (camera paths: keys with `p` / `l` / `lens` / `roll` / `t`, `ease`, `shake`; entity-relative points `{rel: 'player' | npcId, off: [right, up, forward]}`; `fx` = `dof {focus, fstop}`, `mb`, `streak`, `grade`, `light`, `ev`, `bloom`, `eyelid`, `blur`, `flash`, all keyable over the shot with `[{u, v}]`; `hours: [from, to]` drives the prison clock; `releaseAt`; `walk` = scripted path of the player stand-in; `sim.speed`), `src/director.js` (what the page does: `stage` options `hour, released, area, env, outside, player, npcs, show, hide, humans, lights, advance, js`), `tools/cine/render.mjs`, `tools/cine/trailer/look.mjs` (shared looks: `look('dawn')`, `'warm'`, `'tunnel'`, `'gold'`, `'alarm'`), and the scouting files `tools/cine/scout-*.mjs` (working examples).
* **Bot-driven shots** replay the real deterministic playthrough and put the camera anywhere (or `cam: 'player'` for the game's own first-person view, `ui: true` to keep the HUD): `sim: { bot: 'shim' | 'alarm' | 'keycard' | 'bribe', at: <sim seconds> }`. Event timeline (sim seconds, shim route): intro cinematic 0-34; count 35-55 (doors released 55); chow 97-120; commissary 126; Cutter dialogue 127-158; mop 158-178; Intake 188; shim at the reader 191-199; Records 199-226 (ledger copied 212); chapel 285; Harrington 290-304; font cinematic 309-324; tunnel 329-358; outside 376; clearing 396-422; ending cinematic 422-460. Alarm route is identical to 199 s, then: camera spots the player 204, Pruitt chases 204-208, arrest 208, back in the cell 210-218. Full data (player position / area / clock every 2 s and every bot message): `tools/cine/trailer/data/plan-{shim,alarm,keycard,bribe}.json`. In bot shots the player is at a known place, so use `rel: 'player'` cameras; the double (`double: true`) is the `maddox` NPC mirroring the player.
* World facts: `tools/cine/trailer/data/marks.json` (named positions: font, tunnel start / foot / corner / mouth, clearing, oak, crate, blanket, camera, ledger, desks ... and every NPC's place); areas (x0, z0, x1, z1): hall -18.6..18.6 x -10.6..10.6 (12 m high, two tiers, glass watch booth in the middle, ROUTINE board on the east wall at z -3.6, 12 cells per row along north and south walls, clerestory windows at y 8.4), eastCorr 18.3..50.4 x -2.4..2.4, canteen 24..42.4 x -16.6..-2, intake 29.7..44.6 x 2..10.4, records 44..52.2 x 2..10.4 (camera at 51.4, 3.0, 2.6; ledger 48.3, 0.95, 5.9; a TRANSFER ORDER sign on a wall), westCorr -50.4..-18.3, library -40.4..-27.6 x -12.6..-2, chapel -72.4..-49.8 x -10.6..10.6 (stained glass in the north wall, altar x -70.9, font at -55, 0.9, -5.75), tunnel1 x -56.4..-53.6, z -25.4..-4.9 and tunnel2 z -25.4..-22.6 (floor y -3.2, ceiling about -0.45; steps from z -5.4 down to z -9.6; eye height about -1.7), outside from x -60 westwards (gully start -78.5, -3.4, -24; clearing -102, 0, -32; oak -105.5, 0, -35; crate -104, 0.5, -33.6; blanket -105.4, 0.2, -33.1; gold hour: stage `outside: 'gold'`, `env: 'outsideGold'`, hour 14.5). `setActiveArea(area)` shows the area and its direct neighbours only.
* Lessons from scouting: the hall is dark at count (hour 6.4), so use hours 7.5-9.5 for daylight shafts (the clerestory spot and shafts switch on with daylight) and the `light` / `ev` fill; a camera below y 0 or inside a wall shows a blank texture; the first-person hands appear in bot shots unless the shot is third-person (director cameras hide them automatically); the Records ceiling lamp blows out if you aim at it; chapel colour pools are strongest at hour 13.4; the empty mess hall (`humans: 'hide'`) reads beautifully; depth of field with `fstop` 1.6-2.8 and lens 50-85 gives real bokeh; `streak` adds anamorphic flares on practical lights; keep `mb: 1` for camera moves faster than a walk. 85 mm face close-ups are unusable (see rule 1).

## What a good shot is

Check each shot's three scout stills: composed (thirds, leading lines, foreground layers), exposed (no crushed blacks, no blown lamps), nothing intersecting (camera in a wall, a prop clipping), focus on the subject, no human faces or crowds, a clear reason for the move, smooth motion (no jerks; camera speed believable), the look consistent with the neighbours, and the shot reads in the 1-3 beats it has. Reject and rebuild any shot that does not clear all of these.
