# THE 5 COLD EMAIL PRINCIPLES

Read these before you send anything. They're the DNA of every template that follows.

**Principle 1: Research or Die**
The difference between a 0.5% reply rate and a 15% reply rate is usually one thing: evidence that you know who you're emailing. One specific observation about their business (something you actually noticed, not a generic compliment) signals you're not spamming 1,000 people.

**Principle 2: Lead with Value, Not Your Ask**
"I can help you do X" performs worse than "I noticed you're missing X — here's a quick thought on it." Give something first. A relevant insight, a resource, a specific observation. The ask comes after you've earned the click.

**Principle 3: Short Wins. Always.**
Every word you add is another chance for them to stop reading. Target under 150 words. If you're hitting 200+, you haven't thought hard enough about what actually matters.

**Principle 4: One CTA. One Ask. One Easy Yes.**
Don't ask them to: reply, book a call, check out your portfolio, and tell you if they're interested. Pick ONE. A yes/no question is the easiest CTA — "Would it be worth a 20-minute call?" has higher conversion than "Let me know if you're interested."

**Principle 5: The Breakup Email Closes Deals**
The final email in your sequence — "I'll stop reaching out after this" — routinely generates more replies than any other email. People respond to finality. Don't skip it.

## Template 1: The Direct Pitch (Service-Specific)
**Use when:** You've identified a prospect who likely needs your exact service. You've done basic research on their business.

**Subject lines:**
- Option A: Quick question about [COMPANY]'s [SPECIFIC AREA]
- Option B: [YOUR NICHE] for [COMPANY NAME]
- Option C: Noticed something on [COMPANY]'s [website/content/ads]

---

Hi [NAME],

I was looking at [COMPANY]'s [website/content/LinkedIn/ads] and noticed [SPECIFIC OBSERVATION — one genuine thing, e.g., "your blog hasn't been updated since March" or "your Facebook ads are heavy on features but light on social proof"].

I'm a [YOUR NICHE] freelancer who helps [TYPE OF COMPANY] with [SPECIFIC OUTCOME]. I recently helped [SIMILAR CLIENT TYPE] [SPECIFIC RESULT — e.g., "go from 3 blog posts/month to 12 without adding headcount"].

Worth a 20-minute call to see if there's a fit?

[YOUR NAME]
[LINK TO PORTFOLIO/CALENDAR]

---
**Note:** The observation in line 1 is everything. It must be real and specific. "I really love your content" doesn't count. "Your About page hasn't been updated since you hired a new CEO in January" does.

## Template 2: The Problem-First Pitch
**Use when:** You've identified a specific, fixable problem in their business that you can solve.

**Subject lines:**
- Option A: Found an issue on [COMPANY]'s site
- Option B: Quick heads up for [NAME]
- Option C: [SPECIFIC PROBLEM] on your [WEBSITE/ADS/CONTENT]

---

Hi [NAME],

While researching [INDUSTRY] companies, I came across [COMPANY] and noticed [SPECIFIC PROBLEM — e.g., "your site isn't indexed properly on mobile" / "your email opt-in has a broken confirmation link" / "your top competitor is outranking you on 3 high-value keywords"].

This is something I fix for [TYPE OF CLIENT] — usually [QUICK WIN — e.g., "a quick technical SEO audit catches 80% of these in under a week"].

Not a pitch — just thought you'd want to know. If you'd like me to dig into the full picture, happy to share what I find.

[YOUR NAME]

---
**Note:** This only works if the problem is 100% real and you can explain it clearly. Don't invent problems. Do quick research and find one genuine issue — it shows up in 2-3 minutes of looking.

## Template 17: Second Follow-Up (7 Days, Still Nothing)
**Use when:** Day 7, still no reply. Add new value.

**Subject lines:**
- Option A: One more thing, [NAME]
- Option B: [NEW RELEVANT THING] for [COMPANY]
- Option C: Thought of you when I saw this

---

Hi [NAME],

Following up one more time — I came across [RELEVANT RESOURCE / INSIGHT / ARTICLE] that felt relevant to what you're doing at [COMPANY]: [LINK or one-line insight].

[YOUR NAME]

---
**Note:** Adding value in your second follow-up (a resource, insight, or article) keeps it from feeling like pure pestering.

## Template 18: The Breakup Email (Final Touch, Day 14)
**Use when:** Your last email in the sequence. This often generates the highest reply rate.

**Subject lines:**
- Option A: Closing the loop, [NAME]
- Option B: Last note from me
- Option C: Should I stop reaching out?

---

Hi [NAME],

I'll make this my last note — I don't want to clog your inbox.

If [ORIGINAL OFFER] isn't relevant right now, totally understood. If things change down the line, you know where to find me: [YOUR EMAIL/WEBSITE].

Wishing [COMPANY] the best regardless.

[YOUR NAME]

---
**Note:** The "I'll stop" signal is what gets people to respond. Many people who didn't reply earlier will respond to this with "sorry for the delay, let's talk." Don't skip this email.

# QUICK-START CHECKLIST

Before your first outreach campaign:

**✅ Prep work (one time)**
- [ ] Define your ideal client (industry, size, role you contact)
- [ ] Research 20-30 specific targets (name, company, something specific about each)
- [ ] Set up your email signature with portfolio link
- [ ] Connect Calendly or scheduling link
- [ ] Create your follow-up reminders (calendar or CRM)

**✅ Per email (every send)**
- [ ] Personalized first line (specific to this person/company)
- [ ] Under 150 words
- [ ] One clear CTA
- [ ] Subject line chosen and tested with 1 variation
- [ ] Proofread once — no typos

**✅ Campaign hygiene**
- [ ] Send at most 20-30 cold emails/day (deliverability)
- [ ] Wait 24+ hours between emails to the same domain
- [ ] Track opens/replies in a simple spreadsheet or CRM
- [ ] Follow up consistently — no one-and-done outreach
- [ ] Log every reply, even rejections (they have timing patterns)

**Your target metrics:**
- Open rate: aim for 40%+ (subject line quality)
- Reply rate: aim for 8-15% (body + offer quality)
- Positive reply rate: aim for 3-7% (targeting quality)

If you're below these: fix your targeting before tweaking copy.
